WHAT (1) · Defining and recognising bullying

Bullying in the workplace is an act of unreasonable and continuous behaviour that puts the worker’s health at risk (Kollerová et al., 2023). At schools, the concept of bullying may be considered any aggressive behavior that can be caused by any of the following: the students, parents, or professionals working in the same institution (La Trobe University, 2019). However, the concept of cyberbullying is different from traditional bullying in the sense that it involves the usage of technology like e-mail, social networks, and chatrooms to bully someone during his/her out-of-office time. Bullying at workplaces might have adverse effects on the teacher’s self-respect and dignity in class.

It is observed through research that the teachers are also subject to bullying behavior from their students, parents, and other colleagues within the workplace setting. One thing that is crucial in terms of affecting the wellbeing of the teacher is the continuous breakdown of the emotional security and effectiveness in their profession due to the aggression in the workplace environment (Khairallah et al., 2023). The bullying of the teachers from their students, parents, and other fellow employees might have an impact on them through causing them anxiety and emotional fatigue (Ozkilic & Kartal, 2012). What is common about workplace bullying and domestic abuse is that both types of behaviors include the element of coercive control aimed at taking away the sense of autonomy and freedom from the victim. However, these types of experiences are not the same. The point is that they both use the same power-and-control mechanisms to make the targeted individuals feel psychologically entrapped and unable to find ways out of the problem situation (Champions of Change Coalition, 2020; AlBahsh, 2023).

One possible reason for the limited research is the intense professional stigma and fear of career reprisal that forces many educators to suffer in silence rather than report administrators or parents. A further explanation may be that institutional leaders often rebrand systemic bullying as individual performance management failures or poor personal resilience. Teachers may be reluctant to report bullying because they fear being labeled as incompetent or damaging their future employment prospects within insular school hierarchies (Zheng et al., 2024). Another reason this issue may remain hidden is that institutional protectionism often blocks external oversight, leaving incidents profoundly underreported and unexamined.

From the perspective of individuals, the impacts of workplace bullying could lead to the breakdown of all coping strategies and psychological resilience of a teacher (AlBahsh, 2023). The negative impact may reach other staff members due to systemic toxicity creating an environment of fear and silence around the school's staff room (Kollerová et al., 2023). On an organizational level, there may be organizational culture fragmentation and a high level of staff turnover. If a teacher suffers from more than one type of abuse, including workplace bullying and domestic violence (Champions of Change Coalition, 2020), the consequences will be even worse due to the lack of safe places for the person.

A published example illustrating this issue is documented in the news report regarding institutional retaliation after formally reporting workplace misconduct (Sanders, 2025). The report describes allegations of retaliation, rather than an established finding of wrongdoing. The example is significant because it raises concerns about how administrative responses may affect teacher safety and willingness to report workplace misconduct. This case can be linked to the research by Ozkilic and Kartal (2012), which discusses bullying experienced by teachers.

WHAT (2) · Impact on teachers and school culture

The proposed school policy relevant to this issue is the Wellington High School Staff Wellbeing and Anti-Bullying Framework, developed for this hypothetical school. This proposed policy will be examined more closely in the SO WHAT section.

Research suggests that teachers may experience bullying from students, parents, and administrative colleagues across their daily working lives. A significant concern for teacher wellbeing is the continuous erosion of emotional safety and professional efficacy caused by unchecked workplace aggression (Khairallah et al., 2023). Bullying by students, parents, and colleagues may affect teachers by inducing chronic anxiety, demoralization, and severe mental fatigue (Ozkilic & Kartal, 2012). This is important because sustained occupational hostility may contribute to teacher burnout and decisions to leave the profession (Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership [AITSL], 2020).

Family Systems Theory helps explain how stress and tension within relationships may affect other people in a school community (The Bowen Center for the Study of the Family, n.d.). In the relational matrix, Family Systems Theory shows how anxiety and stress permeate through the subsystems, and this makes it hard for institutional leaders to deal with the issue of bullying because the power relations involve students, parents, teachers and administrators.

One such example from the literature is evident from media investigations highlighting the serious behavioral issues and aggressions witnessed against educators (Messenger, 2026). The example is important in its demonstration of the threat of aggression in terms of putting the safety of educators at risk. The importance of the example is highlighted in the fact that it raises questions about how schools respond to workplace safety concerns.

SO WHAT (1) · PROSPER and wellbeing theory

The PROSPER model allows one to view wellbeing from the perspective of positive psychology by considering such core aspects as positivity, relationships, outcomes, strengths, purpose, engagement and resilience (Noble & McGrath, 2015). Developed initially as a means of improving students' mental health, it provides a framework for fostering resilience, academic motivation, and emotional wellbeing in the educational context. With respect to teacher wellbeing, PROSPER can be considered helpful because it changes the emphasis within the institution from compliance to proactive actions that will help teachers demonstrate professional efficacy. Safety at Maslow's level can be related to both physical and psychological protection in the workplace since teachers need stable, safe working conditions to be able to perform their work efficiently (Maslow, 1943). The sense of belonging can be associated with PROSPER because of the importance of positive social relationships in creating the professional community. Lastly, esteem is especially relevant to teacher bullying because bullying in the workplace targets the professional status of the person.

Based on the PROSPER model, the adverse effects of bullying can hinder the establishment of the right relations and engagement necessary for sustainable teaching. As per Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, such behavior goes against safety and self-esteem needs, placing teachers in constant struggle for survival rather than development. In this way, both frameworks illustrate that workplace aggression is much more than an interpersonal problem; it represents a threat to important human needs that may affect professional effectiveness. Constant verbal bullying may negatively influence professional efficiency as it continuously decreases the confidence and self-assurance of the educator. Coercion undermines the sense of autonomy and self-control over one's professional sphere. Cyberbullying is likely to have a significant effect since cyber harassment invades the personal space, which makes teachers unable to leave online abuse out of work time (Education Support, n.d.). Such a situation can make teachers reconsider their career and feel professional demoralization (Ozkilic & Kartal, 2012).

Workplace bullying can contribute to isolation when targeted individuals are ostracized by peers or unsupported by leadership during conflict. In terms of their professional life, a teacher might withdraw from any collaborative planning and other events at the school. However, the negative consequences could be experienced not only at work but also at home since the stress associated with it affects all aspects of life and puts a strain on personal relationships as well as family support. Consequently, an educator may feel helpless and isolated, which could influence their decision to remain in the profession. Family Systems Theory says that a person acts within the framework of a complicated system, in which stress in one subsystem impacts other relationships. In terms of applying Family Systems Theory to a school setting, one can say that an organization is a sort of emotional ecosystem wherein all people depend on each other. The bullying of any staff member might have an impact on the whole system, leading to constant tension, defensiveness, and mistrustful communication between colleagues.

SO WHAT (2) · School policy and support

The proposed Code of Conduct and policy recognises the importance of maintaining professional standards and mutual respect across the school community, drawing on guidance from AITSL (2020). One of the potential advantages of the proposed policy is that it would establish expectations for occupational health and safety standards for all educational staff members. Nevertheless, taking into consideration the PROSPER model, it is important that the proposed policy should not only focus on compliance but also encourage positive wellbeing and relational resilience. One possible limitation would be treating bullying as a single event rather than recognising repeated behaviour and wider cultural problems involving students, parents and colleagues. The proposed policy should also pay attention to the effects of cyberbullying and online harassment. It is important for teacher wellbeing because educators are exposed to potential risks in the digital environment and cannot rely only on traditional procedures.

This case can be understood through PROSPER because the allegations of institutional retaliation documented in media reports raise concerns about positive relationships and organizational trust (Sanders, 2025). Maslow’s hierarchy helps explain why targeted educators may experience distress when their safety and esteem needs are threatened. From a Family Systems Theory perspective, the institutional response may influence the wider relational system and discourage other staff members from raising concerns. The example demonstrates why schools should respond fairly to bullying complaints and provide appropriate support for educators.

NOW WHAT · Professional learning workshop

Professional learning workshop: written discussion

In analyzing the impact of bullying in the workplace, it has become obvious that teacher well-being should get more attention in school environments. Bullying may have various impacts on the teacher. It may undermine their confidence, cause stress, and create an uncomfortable environment for the teacher. In my opinion, although policies are crucial, they cannot solve everything. The teacher needs to realize that they would be listened to and their problems would be considered. Given this, a professional learning workshop at Wellington High School has been suggested. This workshop will concentrate on recognizing bullying in the workplace, developing good staff relationships, and ways of addressing these issues. In my opinion, professional learning should not only include hearing the information. It should provide the opportunity for discussion of the possible situations.

To begin with, the first activity focuses on recognising bullying behaviours. Staff will be divided into small groups and have a discussion about a hypothetical situation. For instance, a staff member may get intimidating emails from one of the parents. Or another member may be receiving constant criticism from one of his/her colleagues. Staff members will have a discussion as to whether this kind of behavior can be considered bullying and how it should be dealt with. This exercise is linked to Maslow's hierarchy of needs (Maslow, 1943). In order for teachers to be able to concentrate on their professional duties, they need to feel safe and secure first. In my opinion, this link is important, since bullying tends to be viewed by people as just a regular dispute between colleagues. The exercise is also relevant to the Relationships and Resilience components of the PROSPER framework (Noble & McGrath, 2015).

Another important area is developing trust between staff members. The second activity uses Family Systems Theory to explore how relationships influence people's experiences (The Bowen Center for the Study of the Family, n.d.). Although this theory focuses on family relationships, its ideas can also help staff consider how problems within a group may affect others. During this activity, teachers will work in pairs or small groups. They will discuss a situation where a colleague has become isolated after experiencing bullying. Participants will consider how they could approach that person, listen to their concerns and offer appropriate support. They will also discuss behaviours that could make the situation worse, such as gossip or dismissing someone's experience. In my opinion, small actions can make a meaningful difference. Asking a colleague if they are okay or taking time to listen may help them feel less alone. However, emotional support should not replace formal action when bullying occurs. School leaders still have a responsibility to respond appropriately. This activity connects with the Positivity and Relationships elements of PROSPER (Noble & McGrath, 2015).

The third activity aims at enabling teachers to handle challenging situations. The activity will be grounded in Bandura’s social learning theory where the process of learning involves observing others (Bandura, 1977). The activity will require teachers to engage in a role play whereby one challenging situation may be receiving offensive messages via the online school communication tool. The participants will practice how to handle the challenging situation professionally, document what occurred, and where they should turn for help. They will observe how another colleague handles the challenging situation in a calm and supporting way. In my opinion, this activity is helpful because while one knows about bullying, handling it requires something different. This activity aligns with the Strengths and Engagement themes of PROSPER (Noble & McGrath, 2015).

First of all, all teachers must be provided with accurate information about how to complain against workplace bullying. All complaint processes must be clear and fair. Besides, consistent response from the administration is also necessary in case teachers want to make a complaint. In the long run, Wellington High School should look into reviewing its proposed anti-bullying policy as well as seeking feedback from its staff members. It would also be helpful if Wellington High School offered professional development sessions to enable its staff members to identify different types of bullying behavior, such as cyberbullying. Anonymous feedback could be valuable in terms of identifying issues that teachers may not want to discuss in an open forum.

Overall, this workshop can be considered a useful approach to enhance the wellbeing of teachers at Wellington High School. The PROSPER model highlights the importance of positive relationships, strengths, and resilience. The hierarchy of needs by Maslow, Family Systems Theory, and the ideas of social learning by Bandura provide some theoretical explanation about the role of safety, relationships and social learning.
